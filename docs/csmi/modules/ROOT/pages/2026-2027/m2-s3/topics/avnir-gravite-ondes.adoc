== Project context

This project supports Avnir Energy's work on monitoring the structural health of nuclear facilities. Together with CEMOSIS, the company has been developing a Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) method for reactor vessels based on high-fidelity modelling of wave propagation in slender structures, whose dimensions are much larger than their wall thickness.

A prototype vessel is approximately 1 m high and 1 m in diameter, with 3 mm thick walls; actual vessels are about 15 times larger.

The numerical method behaves differently on a steel plate approximately 20 cm across and on the prototype vessel. Results for the plate closely match experimental measurements in both amplitude and frequency, whereas the numerical vessel model predicts frequencies that are too high.

The project investigates which modelling assumptions may cease to be valid for the vessel, particularly whether gravity affects the solution. The larger mass may introduce stresses into the wall, changing wave propagation in a way analogous to how tension changes the sound of a drum.

== Objectives

Add the gravity term to the right-hand side of the mechanical dynamics equation and study its effect on the solution.

The stability of the problem must be assessed:

* Can the gravity term simply be added before starting the dynamic simulation?
* Or is an initial static calculation needed to obtain the internal stress distribution and initialise the dynamic calculation?

The second approach generally helps convergence in rapid dynamics problems.

A substantial part of the project will compare numerical results with experimental measurements from the prototype vessel.
