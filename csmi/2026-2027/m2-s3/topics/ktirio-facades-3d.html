3D façade analysis to enrich Ktirio’s building energy models

2. Project context

Ktirio is a platform for simulating building energy performance at neighbourhood and city scales, particularly energy consumption and summer comfort in the context of climate change adaptation. Its models require information about the exterior envelope: dimensions, storeys, glazed areas and opening orientations.

Supervised by CEMOSIS in collaboration with NEPSEN, the project will use textured 3D models and LiDAR data to obtain this information efficiently and enrich the energy models.

NEPSEN proposes three reference sites: Apave in Artigues-près-Bordeaux, Téthys in Bègles and Haut Blanzac in Mérignac. Design and monitoring documents will support validation. Their use will depend on the availability and acquisition dates of the geometry and texture data. Strasbourg data may provide an initial test area.

3. Company partner — NEPSEN

NEPSEN is an independent French engineering and consulting company specialising in the energy and ecological transition. It works with public and private clients on sustainable buildings, renewable energy and industrial energy efficiency. Its services include energy audits, technical studies and project engineering, providing a practical setting for validating the building characteristics extracted for Ktirio.

4. Objectives

Develop a reproducible prototype that can:

  • Load 3D data for a small sample of buildings and identify their façades.

  • Extract rectified façade images, then detect windows using image processing and machine learning.

  • Estimate building heights, storeys and glazed areas; compute the window-to-wall ratio per façade and, where the data allow, per storey.

  • Compare results with manual annotations and reference information provided by NEPSEN.

  • Export the characteristics in a format usable by Ktirio, including their provenance and a quality indicator.

Evaluation will cover accuracy, the proportion of usable façades and processing time. The scope will be adapted to a few hours of work per week, from October to the end of January. Deliverables will include documented code, a report and an oral presentation.

5. Fields

Data and machine learning; modelling, simulation, optimisation and control; high-performance computing; signal and image processing.

6. Mathematical tools

Analytical geometry and coordinate transformations; least-squares and robust estimation of façade planes; projections and image rectification; segmentation and object detection; grouping of openings to estimate storeys; area calculations and error statistics.

Evaluation will distinguish observations from estimates, accounting for occlusions, texture resolution and differences in acquisition dates. A sensitivity study may assess how the extracted parameters affect energy simulations.

7. Computing tools

Python for prototyping, with NumPy/SciPy, OpenCV and PyTorch for image analysis and machine learning; tools for reading and processing meshes and point clouds, such as trimesh, Open3D or PDAL; QGIS and ParaView for inspection and visual validation.

Git/GitHub for version control and reproducibility; JSON or CSV export for integration into Ktirio. GPU use and parallel processing of multiple buildings will be considered as needed.
